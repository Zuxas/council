# The Council -- A User Guide

*From the Chair.*

Welcome. Before you use this council, let me introduce you to the board and
explain how we think. Read this once. It is not a manual of commands; it is a
statement of principles. The commands are elsewhere. What follows is the
character of the institution you are about to convene.

## What the board is

Think of your work as a company.

- You are the **OWNER**. You set direction, you carry the consequences, and you
  make the final call. Nothing here overrides that.
- Bob -- the executor -- is the **FOREMAN**. He builds. He runs the tests, edits
  the files, ships the change. At the gates that matter, he pauses and asks the
  board.
- This council is the **BOARD**. We are independent oversight. We do not build
  and we do not decide. We examine a proposal, argue it honestly among
  ourselves, and hand you a reasoned recommendation. Then you ratify, or you do
  not.

A board that only ever agrees with management is worthless. So is one that
manufactures objection to look busy. We aim to be neither. We weigh evidence,
size ourselves to the stakes, and tell you what we actually found.

## What the board will not do

- **We do not execute.** The board never edits your files, never runs your
  migration, never ships. Recommendation is the whole of our output. Execution
  belongs to the foreman, under your authority.
- **We do not decide.** A verdict is advice with its confidence and its reasons
  attached. You are the ratifier. If our recommendation and your judgment
  diverge, your judgment wins -- that is the design, not a failure of it.
- **We do not pad the panel.** A routine question does not get an
  eight-seat inquisition. We convene the seats the decision earns, and no more.
- **We do not count votes.** Six seats agreeing is not truth. One seat with a
  primary-source citation outweighs a chorus of assertion. The Chair synthesizes
  by evidence tier, never by headcount and never by who argued most gracefully.

## How the board thinks

Every seat owes you more than an opinion. It owes you an argument you can
inspect and, if you choose, tear down. Each seat delivers:

- a **position**, stated plainly;
- **claims**, and every claim is tagged by where it comes from -- drawn from a
  primary source (a file and line, a command, a query), derived by reasoning
  from those sources, or simply asserted. You can see at a glance which parts of
  the argument stand on evidence and which stand on judgment;
- a **warrant** -- why that evidence actually supports the claim, not just that
  it sits near it;
- the **strongest counter** the seat can imagine to its own position, named
  before you have to name it for us;
- a calibrated **confidence**; and
- a **falsifier** -- the specific thing that, if true, would flip the verdict.

That last item is the discipline that keeps us honest. A seat that cannot say
what would change its mind has not been thinking; it has been posturing. We
require the falsifier so that our arguments remain answerable to reality.

## The seats

We keep eight seats, and we select among them per decision. You will rarely see
all eight.

- **Analyst** -- reads the artifact closely; surfaces unstated assumptions; asks
  whether the thing actually says what it is claimed to say.
- **Contrarian** -- must find at least one concrete flaw, or state plainly that
  it found none and exactly what it checked.
- **Empiricist** -- re-derives every quantitative claim from primary sources, so
  no number rides on trust.
- **Cross-vendor** -- a seat run on a different model family entirely. It exists
  to break same-DNA bias: when every seat shares one lineage, they share one
  blind spot, and this seat is our guard against that.
- **Advocate** -- builds the strongest possible case *for* the proposal. Not a
  cheerleader; a steelman.
- **Red team** -- assumes the proposal is wrong, and attacks: the flaw, its
  second-order effects, the worst case.
- **Historian** -- asks whether we have been here before. Was this tried,
  decided, or failed already? Does it contradict a past verdict?
- **Pragmatist** -- grants that the proposal may be correct and asks the harder
  question: is it *worth it*? What is the opportunity cost, the maintenance
  burden, the simpler alternative we are walking past?

One rule is invariant: **Advocate and Red team are always paired.** We never
hear the case for a thing without also hearing the case against it. A board that
steelmans without an adversary is a sales meeting.

## How the seats stay independent

Independence is not a slogan here; it is enforced by procedure.

- **Blind first round.** The seats argue in parallel, in a single pass, and none
  of them sees another's work. What each seat gives you is its own reasoning,
  uncontaminated by the loudest voice in the room. There is no anchor to drift
  toward because there is nothing to see.
- **Anonymized rebuttal.** Only then do we let the seats read each other -- and
  even then, stripped of identity, presented as Response A, B, C. Each seat
  concedes what it must, holds what survives contact, and aims its sharpest
  counter at the strongest opposing claim. Ideas are judged, not reputations.
- **A different bloodline in the room.** The cross-vendor seat runs on another
  model family so that a shared architecture cannot quietly become a shared
  error.

The result you receive from the Chair is a synthesis by evidence tier: where the
board agreed, where it clashed and why, the blind spots we turned into open
questions for you, any dissent worth preserving, the index of evidence we
actually leaned on, and the falsifiers that would overturn the whole thing.

## Your part

You convene us when the stakes justify it -- an approval that is expensive to
reverse, a claim in dispute, an irreversible operation, a decision where someone
already has a thumb on the scale. You do not convene us for what a single test or
query could settle on its own. Sizing the question to the instrument is itself a
sign of good ownership.

And when we are done, remember what we are. We are counsel, not command. We will
give you our best reasoning, our disagreements intact, and our confidence stated
honestly. The gavel is yours.

-- the Chair
